Why MEIS

Not overwriting a field—remembering who owned the device, and when

In many peer systems, department and install location are flat “current” values. Change them and history disappears; past maintenance and costs become hard to attribute. MEIS stores ownership and location as time intervals, so repairs, maintenance, metrology, PM, and part replacements can be reconciled to the department in charge at that time.

Typical peer approach

Department / location = current fields only. Updates overwrite; historical ops and cost ownership drift.

Dept A → overwritten as Dept B (who was responsible? which cost center? unclear)

MEIS approach

Ownership periods · location periods with start/end. Ledger-linked ops stay attributable by interval.

Policy context

National policy & regulatory backdrop

Hospital medical-equipment management is moving from “registers only” to full-lifecycle traceability and clear accountability. MEIS aligns with the following common governance directions (supporting management—not a clause-by-clause certification promise).

Full lifecycle device regulation

Under regulations such as the Regulation on the Supervision and Administration of Medical Devices, providers must keep auditable records across purchase, acceptance, use, maintenance, and disposal. MEIS emphasizes complete master–detail documents and auditable changes.

UDI unique device identification

National UDI programs require linking identifiers with use. MEIS supports UDI-related ledger fields and change history for traceability.

Institutionalized equipment management

Health authorities expect processes for procurement justification, archives, metrology, and adverse-event monitoring. MEIS provides one rich device card plus business sheets for day-to-day work and review packs.

Adverse event monitoring

In-use device events must be recorded and reported. MEIS offers device-linked adverse-event entry and query for QC closure.

Metrology & mandatory calibration

Measuring instruments need periodic verification. Plans, execution, and results sit on the device timeline with readable statuses.

Digitized multi-channel fieldwork

Reviews emphasize process evidence. Shared Web / App / Mini Program capabilities and channel logging reduce paper backfill gaps.

Summary of common industry directions for leadership briefing; binding rules are the statutes and hospital policies in force at review time.

Paper vs digital

Manual registers vs MEIS digitalization

Many hospitals still mix Excel, paper cards, and verbal handoffs. MEIS is not “Excel on the web”—it is one queryable base for ledger, ops, alerts, and audit.

Typical manual / fragmented pain

  • Hard to consolidate: many Excel versions; long reconciliations
  • Silent overwrites: department/location edits erase history
  • Missing detail lines: paper slips and attachments scatter
  • Memory-based reminders: metrology/maintain dues are missed
  • Field vs office lag: late backfill after rounds
  • Last-minute review packs: inconsistent binders under deadline

MEIS digital advantages

  • One record lineage: search, export, ops and archives on-device
  • Interval history + plain change logs: attributable ownership/location
  • Forced master–detail: no empty header-only warehouse/ops docs
  • Due & message alerts: actionable reminders with read/handling trail
  • Multi-channel fieldwork: scan repair/inventory/ops with client channel
  • Always-on evidence: pull by device/period for sampling and accreditation prep
